Slot RTP and Volatility

Every slot has a return to player (RTP) set by the developer and certified by a test lab. It tells you what share of all money bet the game pays back over millions of spins.

What 96% RTP really means

A 96% RTP slot keeps 4% of all money bet over its lifetime. If you bet $1 a spin for 500 spins, you've wagered $500, and the average loss is $20. Your own session could end at $0 or $300, because RTP describes the long run, not your next hour.

Same game, different RTP

Many studios ship one slot in several RTP versions, for example 96.5%, 94% and 92%, and the casino chooses which to run. Open the game's info or paytable screen to see the version you're playing. If two casinos show different numbers for the same slot, the higher one costs you less.

Volatility

Volatility describes how wins are spread. Low volatility slots pay small wins often, so your balance moves gently. High volatility slots pay rarely but bigger, so long losing runs are normal. Two slots with the same RTP can feel completely different.

  • Small budget or long session: low or medium volatility.
  • Chasing a big feature win: high volatility, with a smaller stake per spin.

Myths to ignore

  • "It's due to pay." Each spin is independent. A slot has no memory.
  • "Time of day matters." Certified random number generators don't change by hour.
  • "Bonus buys are better value." Buying a feature usually has a similar RTP; it just spends your money faster.
