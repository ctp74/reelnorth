Free Spins Explained: Max Cashout, Wagering and Expiry

Free spins are slot spins a casino pays for, usually at a fixed bet size on one chosen game, and whatever they win is normally credited as bonus money rather than cash. Whether that money ever reaches your bank account depends on three terms: the wagering requirement on the winnings, the maximum cashout, and the expiry dates.

The word "free" is doing a lot of work in that offer. The spins themselves cost you nothing extra, but the winnings come with conditions that decide how much, if anything, you can withdraw. This guide explains how free spins work, how to work out what a bundle of spins is worth on average, and how each of the three main terms changes that value. The numbers in the examples are round illustrative figures, not any real offer.

Two different things called "free spins"

People use the same phrase for two unrelated things, and mixing them up causes a lot of confusion:

  • Free spins as a slot feature. Many slots have a bonus round, often triggered by scatter symbols, that plays a set number of spins without taking a bet. Any wins are paid straight into your normal balance as cash. This feature is already built into the slot's return to player (RTP), so it doesn't change the game's long-run cost.
  • Free spins as a casino promotion. The casino gives you a number of spins on a particular slot, for signing up, depositing, or as a loyalty reward. These are the ones with terms attached, and they are what the rest of this guide is about.

Promotional free spins come in two main kinds. Some arrive with a deposit, as part of a welcome or reload package. Others need no deposit at all. No-deposit spins often carry stricter terms, because the casino is giving value to people who may never deposit.

How free spins work, step by step

Most promotional free spins follow the same path:

  1. You claim or receive the spins. They are tied to one slot, or a short list of slots, and you can't move them to another game.
  2. Each spin has a fixed value. The casino sets the bet per spin, often a small amount such as 10 or 20 cents. You can't raise it.
  3. You play the spins. Wins from each spin are added up as you go.
  4. The winnings are credited. With most offers they land as bonus money, not cash. With "no-wagering" spins they land as cash.
  5. You meet the conditions. Bonus money usually has to be wagered a set number of times before it converts to cash, within a deadline, and only up to a maximum cashout.

Steps 4 and 5 are where the real value of the offer is decided. Two offers with the same number of spins can be worth very different amounts once you read the terms.

What free spins are worth: spin value and RTP

Start with the face value: the number of spins multiplied by the value of each spin. Then multiply by the slot's RTP to get the average amount the spins will win. On a slot with a 96% RTP, each dollar of spins returns 96 cents on average over a very large number of spins.

The spin count on its own tells you very little. Twenty spins at $1 are worth twice as much as 100 spins at 10 cents, even though the second offer sounds five times bigger. Always look for the value per spin in the terms.

The game matters too. The same $10 of spins averages $9.40 on a 94% RTP slot and $9.70 on a 97% one. And remember that the average hides a wide spread. On a high-volatility slot, a short run of 50 spins often wins very little, and occasionally wins a lot. Wagering requirements on free spin winnings

Wagering on free spins is usually set as a multiple of the winnings, not of the face value. If your spins win $9.60 and the terms say 35x, you need to place 35 × $9.60 = $336 in bets before the balance turns into withdrawable cash. Your actual winnings will vary from spin to spin, so the requirement changes with them; the examples below use the $9.60 average from a $10 bundle of spins on a 96% RTP slot.

Every bet you place to clear wagering carries the house edge. At 96% RTP, the average cost is 4 cents per dollar bet, so $336 of wagering costs about $13.44 on average, which is more than the $9.60 you started with.

The free spins themselves don't use your own money, so a negative figure doesn't mean you owe anything; it means the wagering is expected to use up more than the winnings, and the balance often runs out before the playthrough is finished.

This is why a small number of no-wagering spins can be worth more in practice than a big bundle with high wagering. The general rules on game weighting, maximum bet size and what counts towards playthrough are the same as for any bonus; our wagering requirements guide covers them in detail.

Max cashout: the cap on what you can withdraw

Many free spin offers, and no-deposit offers in particular, set a maximum cashout. This is the most you can convert from the bonus into withdrawable cash, no matter how much you win. Anything above the cap is removed when the bonus converts.

Worked example (illustrative numbers). Say your spins win $300, you complete the wagering and finish with a bonus balance of $250, and the offer has a $100 max cashout. You can withdraw $100. The other $150 is removed from your balance.

A cap matters more than it looks. A slot's average return includes the rare big wins. When the terms cut off everything above a set amount, those big wins can't count in full, so the real average value of the spins is lower than the face value times the RTP. The tighter the cap, the more value it removes, and the more it hurts on high-volatility games where most of the return comes from occasional large hits.

Check also whether a minimum withdrawal amount or a required deposit applies before free spin winnings can be cashed out. Some offers need a deposit first, even if the spins themselves needed none.

Expiry: the deadlines on free spins

Free spins usually come with more than one clock. The exact periods vary by offer, so read the terms for each one:

  • Claim window: how long you have to accept the offer or opt in.
  • Spin expiry: how long you have to use the spins once they are credited. Unused spins are lost.
  • Wagering deadline: how long you have to complete the playthrough on the winnings. If time runs out, the bonus balance and any unfinished progress are removed.

Short deadlines push you to bet faster and in bigger amounts than you might otherwise choose. That is the opposite of how to keep gambling affordable. If a deadline would mean playing more than your normal budget allows, it is better to let the offer go.

A quick checklist before you use free spins

  • What is each spin worth, and what is the total face value?
  • Which slot are the spins on, and what is its RTP?
  • Are winnings paid as cash, or as bonus money with wagering? If wagering, how many times, and on what amount?
  • Is there a maximum cashout, and how does it compare with the face value?
  • Which games count towards wagering, and is there a maximum bet while the bonus is active?
  • How long do you have to use the spins and to finish the wagering?

Free spins don't change the maths of the slot. No strategy removes the house edge, and that is just as true when you are playing with bonus money. The best you can do is pick offers with fair terms and treat any winnings as a pleasant surprise. For how slots compare with table games on cost, see what the house edge is on every casino game.

Common mistakes with free spins

  • Judging an offer by the number of spins. The value per spin matters more than the count.
  • Skipping the terms. Wagering, max cashout and expiry decide what the spins are worth, and they are only in the small print.
  • Breaking the maximum bet rule. One bet over the limit while a bonus is active can cancel the winnings.
  • Depositing to chase a capped balance. If the most you can withdraw is a small amount, adding your own money to keep playing rarely makes sense.
  • Racing a deadline. Bigger, faster bets to finish wagering in time raise both your spending and your risk of breaking the bet limit.
  • Treating winnings as income. Free spin winnings are a bonus, not a reason to play more than you planned.

When to walk away

Decide on your budget before any offer, and don't let free spins change it. Walk away if the terms would have you bet more than you planned to clear a bonus, if you notice yourself depositing to "unlock" winnings that are capped anyway, or if you're playing on past the time you set.
