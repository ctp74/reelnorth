What Is the House Edge? Every Casino Game Compared

The house edge is the share of every bet that a casino game keeps on average over the long run: a 2.7% edge means you lose about $2.70 for every $100 you bet. It ranges from under 1% on blackjack, baccarat and good video poker to well over 10% on many side bets, so the game and bet you choose matter far more than any system.

This guide explains where the house edge comes from, compares the main bets in every common casino game, and shows how to turn a percentage into a realistic cost per hour. One thing up front: no strategy removes the house edge. The right choices can make it smaller; nothing makes it disappear.

What the house edge means

Every casino bet pays a little less than it would if the game were fair. Take a single number on a European roulette wheel. There are 37 pockets, so a fair payout would be 36 to 1. The casino pays 35 to 1. Across all 37 possible results, you win $35 once and lose $1 thirty-six times, a net loss of $1 for every $37 staked. That is 1 ÷ 37 = 2.70%, the house edge.

Three ways to say the same thing:

  • House edge: the average loss as a percentage of each bet (2.70%).
  • Return to player (RTP): the average amount paid back, which is 100% minus the house edge (97.30%). Slots usually quote RTP instead of house edge.
  • Expected loss: the edge applied to your actual bets. $1,000 bet at 2.70% is an expected loss of $27.

The key word is average. The house edge says nothing certain about tonight. You might win, lose a little or lose a lot. What it does tell you is the direction your results drift the longer you play, and how quickly.

Game by game: what moves the edge

Blackjack

Blackjack's low edge depends on you. Basic strategy, the mathematically best play for every hand, brings it to about 0.5% in a standard game. Playing on hunches can push it several times higher. Rules matter too: a table that pays 6:5 for blackjack instead of 3:2 adds about 1.4%, more than doubling your cost. Our blackjack basic strategy guide has the full chart.

Baccarat

Baccarat needs no decisions. Banker wins slightly more often than player (45.86% against 44.62% of hands, with ties the rest), which is why the casino takes a 5% commission on banker wins. Even after that commission, banker is the cheapest bet at 1.06%. The tie bet pays 8 to 1 but only hits about 9.5% of the time, which makes it one of the worst bets in the building.

Craps

Craps looks complicated, but the best bets are the plain ones. Pass line (1.41%) and don't pass (1.36%) are among the lowest edges available. Once a point is set, you can usually add an "odds" bet behind your line bet that pays true odds and carries no house edge at all. It does not remove the 1.41% on the line bet, but it lowers the average edge across all the money you put on the table. The one-roll bets in the middle of the table, like any seven, carry the highest edges.

Roulette

On a single-zero wheel every bet has the same 2.70% edge, whether you bet red or a single number. The double zero on American wheels nearly doubles it to 5.26%, and the five-number bet (0, 00, 1, 2, 3) is worse still at 7.89%. French tables with la partage return half your even-money stake when zero lands, halving the edge on those bets to 1.35%. See our roulette odds guide for more.

Slots

Slots publish an RTP rather than a house edge, and the same game can ship in several RTP versions. A 96% RTP slot has a 4% house edge; a 92% version of the same game has 8%. Progressive jackpot slots often have lower base RTPs because part of each bet funds the jackpot. Our slot RTP and volatility guide shows how to check which version you are playing.

Video poker

Video poker is unusual because you can read the exact maths from the pay table. "9/6" Jacks or Better (a full house pays 9 and a flush pays 6 per coin) returns 99.54% with perfect strategy, an edge of 0.46%. Machines that cut those two payouts, even by one coin each, return noticeably less. Mistakes in which cards you hold also add to the edge quickly.

Sic bo and keno

In sic bo, small and big are the best bets at 2.78%; the triple bets are 13.89% or worse depending on the payout. Keno pay tables vary so widely that no single figure applies, but its edge is typically among the highest of any casino game. If a game does not show its RTP, treat that as a warning sign.

Why you lose more than the edge suggests

Casinos also talk about "hold": the share of the money you bring that the casino ends up keeping. Hold is usually far higher than the house edge, because you re-bet the same money many times. Bring $100 to a European roulette table and bet a total of $500 over the evening by recycling wins, and the expected loss is $500 × 2.70% = $13.51. That is about 13.5% of the money you started with, not 2.7%. The longer you play, the more of your budget the edge takes.

Why no strategy beats the house edge

Betting systems such as doubling after a loss change the pattern of your results, not the average. Every bet in the sequence carries the same edge, so the total expected loss is still the total amount bet times the edge. A system that bets more in total actually costs more. Past results do not help either: dice, wheels and certified random number generators have no memory, so a "due" number is no more likely than any other.

The choices that genuinely lower your cost are simpler:

  • Choose games and bets from the top of the comparison table.
  • Play correctly where skill matters (blackjack, video poker).
  • Pick better rules: single-zero roulette, 3:2 blackjack, full-pay video poker, higher-RTP slot versions.
  • Bet less in total: smaller stakes, slower games and shorter sessions.

All of these reduce the average cost of playing. None turns it into a profit, and none removes the edge.

Common mistakes

  • Judging a bet by its payout. A 30 to 1 payout sounds generous until you see it carries a 13.89% edge. Big payouts usually mean big edges.
  • Taking insurance at blackjack. It is a separate side bet with roughly 15 times the edge of the main game played well.
  • Ignoring the rules sign. 6:5 blackjack, double-zero roulette and short-pay video poker look the same as the better versions until you read the small print.
  • Forgetting speed. Auto-play, turbo spins and fast table variants multiply the total you bet each hour.
  • Chasing losses. Raising stakes to win back money increases the amount exposed to the edge and makes larger losses more likely.
  • Treating gambling as income. Over time, the house edge means the average player loses. Budget for it as the cost of entertainment.

When to walk away

Decide your budget and time limit before you start, and stop when you reach either one, whether you are up or down. Stop if you notice you are betting more to recover losses, playing longer than planned, or using money meant for something else.
