Craps Odds: The Best and Worst Bets on the Table

The best craps odds come from the simplest bets: pass line or don't pass (about 1.4% house edge), backed with the free "odds" bet, which has no house edge at all. The worst are the one-roll proposition bets in the middle of the table, where the house edge runs from 11% to almost 17%.

Craps has dozens of possible bets, which is why the table looks intimidating. The good news is that you only need to understand a handful of them, and the maths behind every one comes from the same 36 ways two dice can land. This guide shows where every number comes from, ranks the main bets from best to worst, and works through what a typical session costs on average.

Where craps odds come from: the 36 dice combinations

Two six-sided dice can land in 36 equally likely ways. Seven is the most common total because six combinations make it (1-6, 2-5, 3-4, 4-3, 5-2, 6-1). Two and 12 are the rarest, with one combination each.

TotalWays to roll itChance per roll
2 or 121 each2.78%
3 or 112 each5.56%
4 or 103 each8.33%
5 or 94 each11.11%
6 or 85 each13.89%
7616.67%

Every craps bet is a race between some totals and others. The house edge appears when a bet pays less than its true odds. For example, a seven comes up 6 times in 36, so the fair payout on "any seven" would be 5 to 1. The table pays 4 to 1, and that gap is the casino's cut. The best bets in craps

Pass line: 1.41% house edge

The pass line is the bet most players make. On the first roll (the come-out roll), you win on 7 or 11 and lose on 2, 3 or 12. Any other total (4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10) becomes the "point". The shooter then keeps rolling: you win if the point comes again before a 7, and lose if a 7 comes first.

On the come-out roll you win 22.2% of the time, lose 11.1% of the time, and a point is set the other 66.7% of the time. Once a point is set, your chance of winning depends on which point it is:

PointChance the point comes before a 7True odds against
4 or 1033.3%2 to 1
5 or 940.0%3 to 2
6 or 845.5%6 to 5

Put it all together and a pass line bet wins 244 times in 495, or 49.29% of the time. It pays even money, so the house edge is 7/495, or 1.41%.

Don't pass: 1.36% house edge

Don't pass is the mirror image. You win on 2 or 3 on the come-out, lose on 7 or 11, and once a point is set you win if a 7 arrives before the point. A roll of 12 on the come-out is a tie (the "bar 12" rule); without it, the casino would have no edge at all. The result is a slightly lower house edge of 1.36%. Some players feel awkward betting against the shooter, but the maths is marginally better.

Come and don't come: same as pass and don't pass

Come and don't come bets work exactly like pass and don't pass, but you place them after a point is already set. The next roll acts as their own come-out roll. The house edge is the same: 1.41% for come and 1.36% for don't come.

The odds bet: 0% house edge

Once a point is set, you can add an "odds" bet behind your pass or come bet. It pays the true odds shown in the table above: 2 to 1 on 4 or 10, 3 to 2 on 5 or 9, and 6 to 5 on 6 or 8. Because the payout matches the real chance, its expected value is exactly zero. It is one of the very few casino bets with no house edge.

You can't make an odds bet on its own, and it doesn't remove the 1.41% on your line bet. What it does is spread that fixed cost across more money. The table sets a limit, often written as "2x", "3-4-5x" or higher. Here is the combined house edge on everything you put on the table if you always take full odds:

Odds taken behind pass lineCombined house edge
No odds1.41%
1x0.85%
2x0.61%
3-4-5x0.37%
10x0.18%

"3-4-5x" means up to 3 times your line bet on 4 or 10, 4 times on 5 or 9, and 5 times on 6 or 8. Odds bets are paid at true odds; check how the game you play rounds payouts on odd amounts.

A lower percentage doesn't mean you lose less in dollars. Your average dollar loss stays the same, because it comes entirely from the line bet. You simply have more money on the table and a wider range of results. Only take odds you can comfortably afford to lose.

Place 6 and place 8: 1.52% house edge

A place bet on 6 or 8 wins if that number rolls before a 7. It pays 7 to 6 against true odds of 6 to 5, for a house edge of 1.52%. That is close to the pass line, which makes it the one place bet worth knowing. Bet in multiples of $6 so the 7 to 6 payout works out in whole dollars.

The middle-of-the-road bets

  • Place 5 or 9 (pays 7 to 5): 4.00% house edge.
  • Place 4 or 10 (pays 9 to 5): 6.67% house edge.
  • Field bet: a one-roll bet on 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11 or 12. With 2 and 12 paying 2 to 1, the house edge is 5.56%. If the 12 pays 3 to 1 instead, it drops to 2.78%. Check the layout before you bet, because the payout on 12 varies from table to table.
  • Buy and lay bets: these pay true odds on a number but charge a commission. The house edge depends on how the commission is charged. For example, a buy bet on 4 or 10 with 5% charged only when it wins carries a 1.67% edge.

The worst bets in craps

The centre of the table is where the casino earns most of its money. These bets pay big numbers, but the payouts fall well short of the true odds.

Payouts shown are common "to 1" payouts. Some tables quote "for 1" payouts (for example 30 for 1), which include your stake and pay one unit less, so the house edge is higher.

The Big 6 and Big 8 are worth singling out. They win in exactly the same situations as a place bet on 6 or 8, but pay even money instead of 7 to 6. The same outcome costs you 9.09% instead of 1.52%.

Hardway bets win only if the number rolls as a pair (3-3 for hard 6) before a 7 or the "easy" way (such as 4-2 or 5-1). One-roll bets are settled on the very next roll, so they can also burn through a budget quickly.

Worked example: what a craps session costs on average

These are round illustrative numbers, not a prediction of any real session. Suppose you bet $10 on the pass line and always take the maximum 3-4-5x odds.

  • A pass line bet takes about 3.4 rolls on average to settle.
  • Including the odds you add when a point is set, you put an average of about $37.78 into action per pass line decision.
  • Your average loss is about $0.14 per decision, which is 1.41% of the $10 line bet. The odds bet adds nothing to it.

Compare that with putting the same $37.78 straight on the pass line with no odds: the average loss rises to about $0.53. Or stick to the plain $10 pass line and play 100 decisions: the average loss is about $14.14.

Averages hide big swings. Over a short session you could finish well up or well down, and many players lose much more than the average. Plan with your bankroll, not with the averages.

Does any craps strategy beat the house edge?

No. No strategy removes the house edge. Each roll of the dice is independent, so betting patterns, "hot shooter" streaks and progressions such as doubling after a loss do not change the odds of any bet. They change only the size and timing of your bets. Adding up bets that each carry a house edge always gives a total that carries a house edge. The same is true at the roulette wheel, which our roulette odds guide covers in detail.

What you can control is which bets you choose. Sticking to pass, don't pass, come, don't come, odds and place 6 or 8 keeps the cost of play as low as craps allows. Digital craps games use a random number generator in place of physical dice, but the same 36-combination maths applies to a fair game.

Common mistakes at the craps table

  • Chasing big payouts in the middle. A 30 to 1 payout sounds generous, but the true odds are 35 to 1.
  • Betting the Big 6 or Big 8. Place the 6 or 8 instead for the same result at a fraction of the cost.
  • Taking more odds than you can afford. The odds bet is fair, but it still raises how much you can lose on a single roll.
  • Ignoring the field payout on 12. 2 to 1 versus 3 to 1 halves or doubles the house edge.
  • Believing a number is "due". Dice have no memory. A 7 is just as likely after ten rolls without one as on any other roll.

When to walk away

Set a loss limit and a time limit before your first roll, and stop when you reach either one. Walk away if you notice yourself raising bets to win back losses, betting with money meant for bills, or playing longer than planned. Treat craps as paid entertainment.
